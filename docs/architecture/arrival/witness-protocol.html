Specification 04 · tamper evidence

Arrival Witness Protocol

A logical hash chain detects damaged history. A remembered head outside the mutable store detects the harder cases: tail deletion, old-backup rollback, and complete re-chaining.

Security draft v1 External head attestation 26 August 2026
01

Purpose and security boundary

This protocol answers the narrow question: “Can a verifier determine that the Arrival history presented now is not the history it previously accepted?” It works across file, DuckDB, PostgreSQL, and service backends because it commits logical heads rather than physical database bytes.

Necessary trust separation

If one administrator can rewrite the backend, every witness copy, and every witness signing key, that administrator can manufacture a consistent replacement history. Detection requires at least one previously accepted head or trusted key outside the rewrite boundary under consideration.

Relationship to existing witness cursors

The current branch uses WitnessPosition for temporal read cursors. This document defines a different artifact: an Arrival head witness. A read cursor says which prefix to query; a head witness says which prefix a verifier already trusted. Implementations should use distinct type and serialization names even if the human-facing suite retains “Witness Protocol.” Ruled: the code vocabulary is head attestation — “witness” stays the read cursor (decision:design/arrival-suite-review-dispositions §3, Rule 18 glossary discipline).

Why not hash the whole database?

A full file digest can confirm one backup image, but ordinary database maintenance may change pages, indexes, free lists, statistics, and metadata without changing any fact. Conversely, a malicious administrator can rebuild a different database file containing a forged logical history and publish its new digest. Arrival witnesses commit the stable record sequence; optional physical snapshot digests remain useful archive metadata.

02

Threat model and claims

Designed to detect

  • Accidental record corruption
  • Interior edit or deletion
  • Projection-only edit or deletion
  • Tail truncation
  • Replacement by an older backup
  • Complete history rewrite and re-chaining
  • Same-lineage equivocation between clients

Not claimed

  • Preventing a privileged administrator from deleting bytes
  • Recovery when every copy is destroyed
  • Truth of an authored fact’s real-world assertion
  • Detection before any trusted first-contact checkpoint
  • Detection when all witnesses and keys collude
  • Availability of a remote witness
  • Confidentiality of record content
  • Aliases of one store reporting distinct filesystem identity, such as two mounts of one export
  • Journal truncation followed by replay, which realigns recorded line positions

Adversary capabilities

The relevant adversary may have direct SQL or filesystem administration over the authority. They can delete rows, edit bodies, replace head metadata, restore old media, or reconstruct a new internally valid chain. They may not control at least one verifier’s cached receipt, independently operated witness, or pinned witness public key. Network responses may be withheld, reordered, or replayed.

Detection window

A witness proves no more than the most recent head it retained. If heads are witnessed hourly, an attacker may remove commits made after the last receipt without contradicting that receipt. A deployment that promises every acknowledged fact is rollback-detectable must retain a witness receipt for the commit before returning that stronger acknowledgment.

03

What the record chain proves

Wire v1 hashes the JCS-canonical record envelope with rh removed. Each successor commits the previous hash. Given collision resistance, the head hash commits the content and custody metadata of the complete dense prefix back to genesis.

rh[0] = SHA-256( JCS(genesis without "rh") )
rh[n] = SHA-256( JCS(record[n] without "rh") )
record[n].prev = rh[n − 1]

Local verification

  1. Validate genesis placement, lineage claim, founding key shape, signature presence, and record hash.
  2. For ordinal n, require the same lineage, exact ordinal n, and prev == rh[n−1].
  3. Recompute rh[n] from the complete envelope excluding only rh.
  4. Apply record-kind placement rules and, when requested, resolve and verify authorship.
  5. Continue through the captured head; stopping early proves only the prefix actually visited.

The valid-prefix problem

Removing the last records leaves a shorter chain that is internally valid. An administrator can also edit content and recompute every subsequent coordinate hash; authored content signatures may remain valid because they intentionally exclude custody coordinates. Local verification alone cannot know that a different valid chain was presented yesterday. That memory is the witness head.

04

The head attestation

A head attestation is a small signed statement about one observed lineage prefix. It is stored outside the authority or copied to a verifier that will remember it. The logical fields are below — counts are optional corroboration, never required (decision:design/arrival-suite-review-dispositions §2: the attestation’s claim is the prefix, not its projection); the final wire object will need its own golden corpus.

This grammar is deferred (§13). It is the signed upgrade of the unsigned head journal in §04b — which is what the adopted minimum actually ships — rather than an alternative to it: one design at two assurance levels. The shipped records deliberately carry a different type so that this corpus stays unencumbered by an unsigned one.

04b

The shipped head journal

What the adopted minimum ships is a smaller artifact serving §04’s purpose: a local, unsigned, append-only journal, one file per lineage, held in a per-user state root outside every store’s restore boundary. Nothing in it is signed, and the absence of sig, issuer, and key_id is the claim rather than an omission—an empty signature field would advertise a transferability this artifact does not have. Its records carry the serialized type arrival-head-observation, distinct from §04’s arrival-head.

Entries are keyed by lineage, never by path. A path-keyed memory manufactures a fresh first contact per worktree, which is silent re-acceptance through the most ordinary operation there is; a restored old copy opened at a new path still presents its lineage, still finds this journal, and is still classified a rollback. The field-level detail is normative in spec/conformance/SCHEMA.md §11 and the comparison vector family and is not restated here. What follows is the model those vectors pin.

Header, entry, or neither

A journal opens with a header line naming the grammar, protocol, and wire versions its record hashes derive under; every later line is one observation. Lines are classified by shape, never by position, because a reader may not assume the header is line 1: two writers racing to create the same journal each write one, and a doubled header is a tolerated artifact of that race rather than a fault.

Line carriesClassificationRead action
The type marker, no entry kindHeaderRecord the versions; it is not an observation.
The type marker and an entry kindUnclassifiableSkip and record. Never absorb it, never refuse the file.
Anything elseEntry-shapedParse as an observation; skip and record one this build cannot read.

The middle row exists because both two-valued repairs fail in opposite directions. Keying on the type marker alone lets a future entry that happens to carry it be absorbed as a header, silently losing the line that held the epoch maximum; requiring the marker plus the absence of a kind refuses the whole file when a later build’s header carries a kind of its own, which is an uncontracted version-skew refusal. So a line bearing both markers is not classified at all. That is a location claim about this build’s ability to read the line, not a verdict about what the line is—which is precisely what this build cannot know.

A journal holding entries but no header is tolerated on the same reasoning. The entries are self-describing evidence that heads were accepted, so what is lost—the versions those hashes derive under—weakens the read rather than voiding the file. The absence is reported without a line number, because an absence does not have one, and it is one of the two things that make a read incomplete.

What an incomplete read costs

Every line this build cannot use is skipped and reported, wherever in the file it sits. A non-empty skip record is the condition this document calls an incomplete read. An incomplete read never refuses the journal; it weakens what the journal can be asked, and §07 carries the four resulting states and the comparisons each one still supports.

Position-bound trust resets

A trust-reset entry (§11) carries a required follows: the physical line ordinal of the entry it was appended after, together with that entry’s identity. The line is the file’s own, counted from 1 over every line including the ones this build skipped—never an index among the entries that happened to parse. An index is a judgment, and a later build that classified one line differently would renumber every entry after it and silently void every reset bound below.

An entry is only bytes in an append-only file, so an old reset re-appended would otherwise re-open the epoch it had closed. Binding to the predecessor’s identity alone does not close that: replaying the predecessor and the reset together as an ordered suffix reproduces the recorded identity exactly. The position cannot be reproduced. Copied bytes appended later always land at a later line, so the two-line replay, a full-suffix replay, and a wholesale journal concatenation all fail one check for one reason. Identity stays in the pair because position alone would accept a truncation that happened to realign a different entry onto the recorded line.

A reset whose binding does not match is not an epoch boundary, and not a refusal. It is skipped and recorded through the ordinary channel, and the scan keeps walking back for an earlier valid one; refusing would brick every later open on a benign crash-retry duplicate, where the same reset is appended twice and the second copy’s predecessor is the first. The direction of the error is the safe one either way—misjudging a legitimate reset leaves K at the higher abandoned head, so the failure is a refusal rather than an acceptance. And the skip record is itself the condition that weakens the read to a lower bound, so a journal holding a replayed reset still refuses everything below that bound, which is the attack the binding was closing.

The ceremony reads the journal’s tail judgment-free—last readable entry and its line, with no epoch scoping, no equivocation check, and no head computation—because a recovery path that depended on the read it recovers from would be unusable at exactly the moment it is needed, which is a journal already equivocating. The position claim then buys its protection with an obligation: there is no lock across read-tail-then-append, so an automated append landing in that window leaves a freshly written, legitimate reset carrying a position that is already stale. The ceremony therefore reads its own entry back and raises TrustResetNotHonored when it did not take effect. Nothing is lost and nothing is wrongly accepted in that case—the previous head still stands and opens keep refusing—but an operator who believes their store will now open is holding a false belief the next open would contradict, and silence is the only unsafe part.

The residual, stated rather than implied: truncating the journal and then replaying realigns every position, and nothing here detects it. That is the known bound of unsigned local state—the same class as deleting the journal outright and being met with first contact—and §04’s chained signed entries are the named upgrade.

A repeated line is a re-assertion

A line whose stripped text duplicates an earlier line in the same file is skipped and recorded before the head is computed: it re-asserts an observation the journal already holds rather than recording a new one, and a replayed line cannot count twice. The rule is type-agnostic—advances, audits, and resets pass one gate—and order-agnostic, so it survives the non-ascending write order §07 describes. It is read-side only: no record shape, producer, or locking changes. Header lines never enter it, which is why the create-race duplicate above is tolerated rather than deduplicated.

Why bytes, and why before K

K is the maximum ordinal of the current epoch, so a replayed historical advance would be taken as current and would name a head an operator had deliberately abandoned. A store restored from the pre-reset backup then presents exactly that head, and “unchanged” carries no descent check by design—so ceremonially abandoned state would be re-accepted in silence. Literal replay of existing bytes is the unsigned threat model, and byte identity is complete against it. A collision from a genuine re-assertion costs only the skip, which lowers K—and so fails on the refusal side, which is the side this must fail on.

First contact and the object behind a name

A location is bound to its lineage when first witnessed, and the binding key is a resolved path—relative segments made absolute, .. collapsed, symlinks followed—so that two vertices reaching one log by different routes present one key. That key is a spelling, not an identity. Resolving a path normalizes it; it does not establish which filesystem object the path names. A case-variant spelling on a case-insensitive filesystem, or a second mount of the same volume, reaches the very same store through a string that resolution leaves untouched and therefore still distinct from the one already bound.

So before a presenting location with no binding of its own is minted as first contact, each recorded binding is live-stat’d and its device and inode compared. A match routes the open through the existing binding, where a replacement bearing a fresh lineage meets a lineage-replaced refusal instead of walking past it. That identity is compared live and never recorded: inodes go stale when a file is recreated and are recycled onto unrelated files, so a stored one is a claim that rots into a false match. Asking the filesystem which object a name currently denotes is legitimate here because the filesystem namespace is ambient authority—two names for one object is a property of the namespace, not of the store—and the answer is a location claim about this host rather than a verdict about the store.

05

Issuance, acknowledgment, and receipts

A witness first verifies that the presented record at ordinal recomputes to record_hash. When advancing from a prior head, it verifies the entire suffix from that prior head to the new one. It derives counts from verified records or from incrementally maintained state tied to the prior attestation.

  1. The authority durably commits and returns the new logical head.
  2. The witness compares that head to its last accepted attestation.
  3. The witness verifies the descendant suffix and updates counts.
  4. The witness creates, signs, and durably retains the next attestation linked by previous.
  5. The caller receives a portable receipt or receipt identifier and caches the accepted head.

Two acknowledgment strengths

StatusMeaningRollback exposure
committedThe authority’s durability profile is satisfied.Changes since the last external receipt may be lost without contradicting a witness.
witnessedAt least the configured witness quorum durably retained this exact head.Rollback below or rewrite through this head is detectable by that quorum.

Personal deployments may choose asynchronous witnessing and accept a small window. Team systems can require witnessed before returning a final write receipt. The API must name which status it returned rather than collapse them into “saved.”

Unsigned memory is still useful

A client that securely caches a previously seen Head can detect rollback without a signing service. Signatures make receipts transferable and independently verifiable; they do not create the fundamental comparison. The cache must remain outside the authority’s rewrite boundary and must be scoped to the lineage.

06

Witness topologies

Personal baseline

Client head cache

Store the last accepted head in an OS keychain or configuration state separate from the DuckDB file. Simple and sufficient for later local edit detection.

Personal stronger

Append-only receipt journal

Chain signed attestations in a separately backed-up journal. This preserves head history and makes accidental cache reset visible.

Team baseline

Independent notary

A small service under separate credentials retains each team lineage head. The database administrator cannot silently rewrite both systems.

Higher assurance

Quorum + gossip

Several witnesses sign heads and clients exchange receipts. Equivocation requires collusion or becomes visible as incompatible signed branches.

Suggested initial choices

For a personal DuckDB canonical store, begin with a keychain-cached head plus a signed receipt copied into ordinary backups. For a PostgreSQL team service, place a witness service in a separate credential and storage domain, and require one witness receipt per acknowledged append group or short interval. Multi-witness quorum is an upgrade, not a prerequisite for the architecture.

Do not colocate the only evidence

A witness table in the same database, writable by the same administrator, is useful operational history but does not meet the administrator-tamper threat model. It may be one copy; it cannot be the only copy.

07

Head comparison state machine

Let K be the head this verifier already accepted and P the head now presented by the authority. Comparison uses lineage, ordinal, and record hash—never timestamp alone.

K is the maximum-ordinal entry of the current trust epoch, and both halves of that carry weight. Not the last entry written: appends are serialized, journal writes are not, so two writers can leave a file holding ordinals 90, 92, 91 in that order. Last-write semantics would read K = 91 there and call a genuine restore to 91 unchanged. Duplicate entries at the maximum are harmless; two entries at the maximum with different record hashes are equivocation, and the journal has recorded both rather than choosing between them.

Not unscoped, either. A trust epoch is the last honored trust-reset entry and everything after it, inclusive of the reset entry itself, which carries the head the operator accepted—scoping it out would leave a just-reset journal with an empty epoch and a next open classifying first contact. Omitting the epoch altogether is the mirror deadlock: after an operator accepts a restore from ordinal 100 back to 90, the abandoned entries at 91–100 still hold the maximum ordinal, so an unscoped K would name the head that was deliberately abandoned and every later open would refuse a rollback forever. Those entries stay in the journal as evidence, not as claims. Equivocation is scoped the same way, so a ceremony can take effect over a conflict the operator has already resolved.

How much of a head the journal has

K is not head-or-nothing. Reading the journal (§04b) yields one of four states, and the weaker ones must not be collapsed into the others: a journal holding content none of which can be read says something quite different from an empty one, and trusting a store on first use because its journal became unreadable is the silent re-acceptance this protocol exists to prevent.

Read stateWhenWhat it supports
Established headThe read was complete—nothing skipped, header presentAll seven comparisons below.
Head lower bound—at least thisThe read was incompleteRollback below the bound; same-height fork at the bound with a differing hash. Equal is not unchanged and higher is not advanced.
Head unreadableContent was claimed and none of it could be readNothing—there is no bound to compare against, rollback included. Not first contact: heads were accepted here.
No headThe journal is absent, or holds only a headerFirst contact.

A read is incomplete when it lacked something it needed: a line that did not parse, a line that could not be classified, an entry written by a later build, or the header. The term names what the read lacked rather than why, so a new cause joins it without new vocabulary. “Incomplete,” never “torn”—damage at the end of the file is not a privileged case. Because journal writes do not ascend in file order, damage anywhere can take the epoch maximum, so a loss mid-file yields a lower bound by construction rather than by concession.

Nothing bounds the accepted head from above, and that asymmetry is the trap. A presented head equal to the bound may be a rollback from the very entry the read missed; one above the bound may still sit below a lost entry higher still, and descent verified from the bound says nothing about an entry that was never on the walk. Gathering more evidence from the store repairs neither, because the missing fact is about what this machine previously accepted, which the store never knew. Recovering it is operator work: repair the journal, or run the trust-reset ceremony.

Degrade with a label; do not refuse

An incomplete read is permanent—the journal is append-only and a lost line stays lost—so refusing on one would make a single crashed writer a store that never opens again. It would also buy nothing against an adversary, who can delete the journal outright and be met with first contact. An open against a bound therefore fires every refusal the bound still soundly supports and then proceeds under an explicitly labeled degradation that claims no comparison outcome at all. It writes nothing—no journal entry, no binding—so the degradation never launders itself into a memory and the bound never rises on evidence the read did not have.

The seven comparisons

Sound against an established head. Guard order is normative: lineage is judged before any ordinal arithmetic, because heights in two different lineages are unrelated number lines.

Verifier sketchpseudocode
open(journal, presented, source):
  read  = journal.read()            # established | lower bound | unreadable | none
  known = read.established_head()   # INDETERMINATE on the weaker two states
  return compare(known, presented, source)

# the degraded arm: every refusal a bound still supports, then no answer
on INDETERMINATE with lower bound B:
  if presented.ordinal < B.ordinal:
    return ROLLBACK
  if presented.ordinal == B.ordinal and presented.hash != B.hash:
    return FORK
  proceed labeled degraded; claim no outcome, write nothing

compare(known, presented, source):
  if known is None:
    return FIRST_CONTACT
  if presented.lineage != known.lineage:
    return LINEAGE_REPLACED
  if presented.ordinal < known.ordinal:
    return ROLLBACK
  if presented.ordinal == known.ordinal:
    return UNCHANGED if presented.hash == known.hash else FORK

  suffix = source.scan(after=known, through=presented)
  return ADVANCED if verifies_as_descendant(suffix) else FORK

First-contact limitation

A verifier with no cached head or pinned witness cannot distinguish the authentic lineage from a carefully constructed replacement. Migration should therefore emit a bootstrap receipt, and new devices should retrieve a checkpoint from an already trusted device or witness rather than trusting only the database they are meant to audit.

First contact is also a state a verifier can reach by accident, through a store it has in fact seen before under another spelling of the same location. §04b covers the identity check that closes that route before a first contact is minted.

The direct answer

Yes: a manually deleted canonical row computes differently. Interior deletion is caught by density and chain verification; tail deletion is caught by comparing the now-shorter head to the last externally remembered head. 09

Layered audit posture

No single “hash database” command should stand in for all integrity claims. Operations combine four layers with different costs and answers.

Preventive controlsRead-only query users, admission-only writes, filesystem or database roles, backups, and writer fencing reduce accidental and ordinary malicious mutation.
Fast open checksValidate genesis, stored head, adopted head record, projection watermark, and comparison to the most recent cached witness.
Incremental verificationVerify every record after the last fully checked checkpoint, advance counts, and issue the next attestation.
Periodic full auditWalk from genesis, verify authorship as configured, re-derive or compare projections, validate archive exports, and reconcile every witness stream.

Projection agreement

Projection verification compares deterministic expansion of records through a captured head to the materialized rows and their (ordinal, sequence) coordinates. It uses multiset or primary-key-aware comparison so duplicated legitimate rows do not disappear inside set semantics. The report distinguishes missing, extra, changed, and misplaced rows.

Archive snapshot digest

For backup operations, record both the logical head and a SHA-256 digest of the stable snapshot or canonical export bytes. The physical digest verifies the recovered object; the logical head verifies its Arrival identity. A healthy restore should satisfy both.

10

Witness keys, rotation, and trust

Key placement

A witness signing key should live in a different credential boundary from backend administration: an OS keychain not stored beside the DuckDB file, a service identity separate from the PostgreSQL role, hardware-backed storage, or an independent notary. The verifier pins the public key or a chain to an already pinned key outside the ledger being judged.

Rotation

Rotation produces a transition statement signed by the old witness key that names the new key ID and public key, effective after a specific attestation_id. The new key’s first attestation links to the last old-key attestation. Verifiers retain the transition; silently replacing a key in configuration is a trust reset, not rotation.

Compromise

If a witness key is compromised, receipts issued before the compromise remain useful only to verifiers that can establish their observation time or retained copy independently. Rotate, publish a compromise boundary, compare heads across other witnesses and archives, and do not claim that a new key retroactively proves the old receipt stream.

Receipt privacy

A head receipt reveals lineage activity, approximate size, and observation time even without fact content. Services should treat receipts as metadata with an access policy. Counts are optional generally (dispositions §2), and omitting them also serves privacy — projection-count corroboration is then unavailable; lineage, ordinal, and record hash remain required.

Witnesses move forward

Normal operation never decreases a witnessed ordinal and never changes the hash at a witnessed coordinate, and no automatic path may abandon a refusal. Two explicit exceptions exist, and each is a decision an operator makes and records. A repair that intentionally starts a new lineage retains a transition report. And within the same lineage, the trust-reset ceremony (§04b) is the licensed way down: an operator accepting a restore from ordinal 100 back to 90 records the gap—what is being abandoned, what was refused, and why—and opens a new trust epoch that scopes every later comparison. Neither rewrites the old witness into agreement. The abandoned entries stay in the journal as evidence; what moves backward is the read scope, not the history.

12

Scaling verification and the Merkle option

The linear hash chain is the correct v1 primitive: appends are O(1), incremental verification is O(new records), and a full audit is O(history). It directly matches dense Arrival order and the branch’s existing record hashes.

Verified checkpoints

A local verifier may retain a checkpoint containing head, counts, verifier version, and completion status after a full audit. It can then verify only the descendant suffix on routine operation. A checkpoint is trusted only within the storage boundary where it was retained; if stored inside the backend under audit, it is a performance hint rather than independent evidence.

Optional accumulator

If very large remote lineages later need compact inclusion or consistency proofs, a Merkle Mountain Range or transparency-log tree root can be added to head attestations. It should be an optional witnessed accumulator derived from the same ordered record hashes, not a replacement for them. The upgrade must define append proofs, consistency proofs, leaf domain separation, and golden vectors before any security claim.

Do not pre-pay the complexity

A Merkle structure does not solve first contact, witness compromise, authority transfer, or projection tampering by itself. Add it only when measured proof-size or audit-time needs exceed the simple chain plus checkpoints.

13

Conformance and open choices

Required test vectors

  • Golden attestation canonical bytes, digest, Ed25519 signature, and verification
  • Unchanged, descendant, lower-height rollback, same-height fork, and higher non-descendant comparisons
  • Interior edit, interior delete, tail delete, old backup, and full re-chain attacks
  • Fact, batch, tick, and key records proving the attested ordinal is store-wide
  • Incremental fact/tick counts across batch expansion
  • Lost acknowledgment and duplicate issuance under one head
  • Issuer receipt-chain gap, replay, reordering, and conflicting successors
  • Witness key rotation and unpinned-key substitution
  • Projection row delete, duplicate, edit, and coordinate permutation
  • Cross-backend verification of the same logical head

The list above is the deferred signed grammar’s corpus. The shipped journal’s read rules—header classification, incomplete reads, epoch scoping, and the seven comparisons—are already pinned by the comparison vector family (spec/conformance/SCHEMA.md §11).

Keep every attestation, periodic checkpoints, or every externally acknowledged commit.

Recommended minimum — adopted

Adopted as the v1 scope (decision:design/arrival-suite-review-dispositions §1): cache one head outside every authority, emit a bootstrap receipt during migration, compare on every open, and perform a periodic full chain-plus-projection audit. All four dissolve into the one append-only journal of §04b: the remembered head is its highest-ordinal entry, the bootstrap receipt is its first, and an audit entry is an entry. The signed-attestation grammar, notary, and quorum apparatus is deferred until a consumer forces it. That closes the deletion concern without forcing a universal database file hash or a premature transparency-log service.
