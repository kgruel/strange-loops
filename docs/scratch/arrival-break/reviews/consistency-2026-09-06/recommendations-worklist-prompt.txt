1: # Arrival identity decisions — September 6, 2026
2: 
3: Status: working architectural recommendations from the consistency pass. This
4: document distinguishes existing contracts from proposed changes; it does not
5: ratify a new wire format or implement a migration. Read alongside the
6: [contract matrix](consistency-contract-matrix-2026-09-06.md) and
7: [identity audit](consistency-audit-identity-2026-09-06.md).
8: 
9: The guiding decision is to keep identity, evidence about identity, and local
10: ways of finding that evidence separate. An observer can interpret several
11: stores together without those stores acquiring one clock, one authority, or
12: one shared authentication namespace.
13: 
14: ## Existing distinctions to retain
15: 
16: | Concept | Existing evidence / scope | What it does not establish |
17: | --- | --- | --- |
18: | Physical lineage | Genesis opens one ordered history; the Arrival declaration anchor must name that same lineage. | A filesystem location, a global observer, or the identity of every vertex that reads it. |
19: | Full head | `(lineage, ordinal, record_hash)` identifies a custody prefix. | The meanings of its observations or the correctness of projected rows merely claiming its ordinal. |
20: | Projection watermark and generation | The watermark is resolved through custody; generation detects changes to a derived view. | A watermark alone is not history proof; generation is not a durable semantic vertex ID. |
21: | Observer label | Exact string in the fact/envelope under its record-kind profile. | A globally authenticated person, a key fingerprint, or a local pathname. |
22: | Key history | One lineage's verified introductions authorize named keys for named observers at later ordinals. | Global equivalence between equal labels, or automatic revocation of older keys. |
23: | Origin | Opaque provenance label; current local runtime also uses exact nonempty vertex name equality to recognize its ticks. | A globally unique vertex ID or an authentication claim. |
24: | Descriptor binding | Backend-owned location interpretation and witness binding evidence. | Identity inferred from filename suffix, or permission to mutate merely because bytes are writable. |
25: | Aggregate member occurrence | A position in one captured composition with its own read basis. | A new physical lineage, a globally stable occurrence ID, or a single instant shared by members. |

28: `arrival.py:KeyRegistry.keys_valid_at`; `declaration.py:validate_arrival_declaration_anchor`;
29: `arrival_binding.py:BindingIdentity`; `vertex.py:_owns_tick`; and the normative
30: [protocol](../../architecture/arrival/protocol.html),
31: [wire profile](../../architecture/arrival/wire-format.html), and

35: ## D1 — Observer identity survives changes of key and location
36: 
37: **Recommendation:** keep the exact observer label as the protocol's named
38: identity within its explicit verification context. Resolve local signing
39: material through an explicit binding for that observer and signing domain.
40: A key rotation, key relocation, vertex-file move, or display-name change must
41: not implicitly change that binding.
42: 
43: Cross-store authentication needs the source key history and the position at
44: which a claim is checked. Equal `observer="alice"` strings in two lineages
45: alone do not prove the same author. A vertex may group them for its own
46: purposes, but that interpretation should not be reported as cryptographic
47: identity. An application can append an explicit association with provenance
48: when it wants to preserve that conclusion.
49: Such an association remains application evidence; it is not, by itself,
50: cryptographic proof of cross-lineage identity.
51: 
52: This does not require replacing strings with global UUIDs or equating an
53: observer with one public key. The existing protocol supports additional keys
54: under the same observer. Its introduction rule is additive: old keys remain
55: valid under that rule. Selecting a new local signing key and revoking an old
56: key are separate operations; this pass does not invent revocation semantics.
57: 
58: **Bounded next implementation:** specify a provider request containing the
59: explicit observer/domain and the relevant declared identity context. Resolve
60: an opaque key reference internally. Keep FACT, ARRIVAL and tick commitments
61: separate. Preserve operation-fresh credential acquisition and forbid key
62: creation during reads, previews, and signer loading.
63: The provider must expose enough public-key evidence for a path claiming
64: authorized signing to check the binding against captured key history. An
65: arbitrary signer callback or a replacement local key alone does not establish
66: a valid rotation. Ordinary unsigned observations remain legal where the
67: operation's policy permits them.
68: 
69: ## D2 — Preserve existing custody bindings explicitly
70: 
71: **Recommendation:** evolve the filesystem provider toward a persisted mapping
72: from exact observer identity to opaque key references. Keep filenames private
73: to that provider. This removes the need for every legal protocol label to be
74: representable as an exact directory spelling.
75: 
76: The current flat/nested resolver is a compatibility implementation, with
77: deliberate refusal of ambiguous keys and filesystem aliases. Do not weaken
78: those refusals to make a migration convenient. The next provider design must
79: specify:
80: 
81: 1. How an existing flat key is bound to its existing self observer before a
82:    file rename can change the lookup convention.
83: 2. How exact nested identities retain their keys, including distinct labels
84:    that a filesystem would otherwise alias.
85: 3. How conflicting mappings or mismatched public material refuse without
86:    minting, overwriting, or silently choosing a winner.
87: 4. How explicit creation publishes one binding and one winning private key
88:    under concurrent creators, and how interruptions are reconciled.
89: 
90: An encoded filename without a recorded/verifiable binding is not a complete
91: migration plan. No key files or live stores are moved in this pass. Arbitrary
92: label portability on a normalizing filesystem remains unimplemented.
93: 
94: An immediate, smaller correction precedes that design: the SDK currently
95: accepts `CustodyCredentialProvider(key_dir=...)` but ignores the value.
96: Recommend an explicit configuration refusal for non-`None` values until a
97: consistent override is supported, rather than continuing to select default
98: keys silently. This is an API compatibility change to document and test; it
99: does not require a key migration.
100: 
101: ## D3 — Vertex continuity is distinct from residence and declaration version
102: 
103: **Recommendation:** a vertex's continued identity should survive moving its
104: locator and editing its interpretation. Its declaration revision describes
105: how that vertex interprets captured facts. Here “revision” means the bounded
106: declaration documents/anchor and H, not an existing public revision-ID type.
107: Neither physical lineage nor the
108: hash of that revision should automatically serve as the vertex's identity:
109: storeless and multi-store vertices make both substitutions inadequate.
110: 
111: Current support is narrower. Historized documents supply the effective name;
112: runtime ticks use that name as origin. `lang.document.diff_documents` already
113: refuses post-genesis vertex renames as routine edits, and Arrival declaration
114: preparation uses that function. Retain that restriction. Local custody still
115: derives self from the locator stem, so moving a file can change credential
116: selection even when its declared identity stays fixed. Any future semantic
117: rename also has to account for tick ownership by origin; present support is
118: not a durable identity/alias mechanism.
119: 
120: For the next bounded pass, preserve the existing semantic name and make
121: credential binding independent of the locator. Treat runtime identity rename
122: as an explicit continuity operation requiring a separate design. Do not
123: silently translate historical origins or reinterpret a new name as an alias.
124: A future durable vertex identifier needs a recorded association to existing
125: names and a policy for storeless vertices; it is not a prerequisite for fixing
126: the current credential configuration and boundary ambiguity.
127: 
128: ## D4 — Boundary identity must be unambiguous within a runtime identity
129: 
130: **Recommendation for the current wire profile:** refuse declarations where a
131: boundary-bearing loop and the vertex boundary produce indistinguishable local
132: ticks. Validate the effective declaration before planning; validating only
133: the mutable local file would leave adopted history able to bypass the rule.
134: Apply the same validation to new/proposed declarations. Do not silently
135: rename a loop or edit historical ticks.
136: 
137: Conceptually a boundary needs `(vertex identity, boundary role, boundary name)`.
138: The existing tick profile supplies `origin` and `name`, and cannot always
139: distinguish the role. Refusal is a bounded way to make the supported subset
140: unambiguous. Adding a role or a new boundary ID to the wire profile would need
141: an explicit compatibility decision, including old histories that contain
142: collisions. Existing ambiguous histories should remain available for evidence
143: inspection even if runtime execution refuses them.
144: 
145: The refusal predicate must cover both tick production and hydration: current
146: `hydrate_snapshot` looks up every owned tick by loop name, even if that loop
147: has no independent boundary trigger. Test same-named passive, count-triggered,
148: and other boundary forms before deciding which configurations are safe. Share
149: the semantic validator across supported Arrival construction/capture and
150: declaration preparation; do not make it a guard only in one SDK command.
151: 
152: The recent origin guard is retained: foreign and originless ticks are visible
153: evidence but do not consume a local boundary. Event timestamp and receipt
154: position retain their distinct roles; a tick's fact cursor is not a global
155: consumption cutoff.
156: 
157: ## D5 — Declaration edits preserve history; reset must be explicit
158: 
159: **Recommendation:** editing a fold or its presentation appends a new
160: interpretation. It does not rewrite prior facts/ticks or automatically mint a
161: new observer, lineage, or boundary. Capture records the effective declaration
162: used for that operation; a later read may apply a later declaration and reach
163: a different conclusion honestly.
164: 
165: Boundary consumption and state replay need their own continuity policy.
166: Keep an unchanged boundary identity's established consumption edge by
167: default. A request to restart a boundary, remove and later recreate it as a
168: new incarnation, or reinterpret old tick payloads under an incompatible fold
169: must be explicit. The exact restart/incarnation representation is deferred.
170: Do not substitute a declaration hash as a boundary ID: every cosmetic or
171: unrelated edit would then risk reopening consumed observations.
172: 
173: Before broadening runtime rename/reset support, test declaration changes
174: against pending boundaries, reset/carry hydration, and historical reads. This
175: pass recommends the policy; it does not claim the present runtime implements
176: all of those transitions or that an existing schema can express every reset.
177: Current hydration associates historical local ticks with current loop names,
178: so remove/recreate and incompatible same-name edits can reuse an old loop's
179: boundary history. Define compatibility and historical detection before
180: promising continuity for such edits. The conservative interim direction is
181: to refuse ambiguous transitions, not to guess a reset; the predicate and
182: coverage for existing histories remain an explicit design task.
183: 
184: ## D6 — Result identity describes evidence and actions separately
185: 
186: **Recommendation:** keep one outcome vocabulary across public operations, with
187: operation-specific evidence. A known actual Commit is retained; an unknown
188: append outcome is not a refusal or a successful retry. A recovered operation
189: may be complete without the recovering invocation possessing its original
190: Commit. It may also find an already-published cache without writing it.
191: 
192: Read bases, projection observations, durable append receipts, witness state,
193: and artifact-publication state answer different questions. They should be
194: consistently named and serialized, not flattened into a universal success
195: boolean. The matrix defines the intended comparison points and proposed
196: cross-operation checks.
197: 
198: ## Order of work
199: 
200: First repair concrete contract drift and make ambiguous boundary execution
201: refuse with useful evidence. Then implement the explicit custody-binding
202: provider and its compatibility path. Stabilize these with public SDK
203: conformance before retiring the competing legacy entrypoints. Durable vertex
204: IDs, boundary incarnations, revocation, and cross-lineage identity association
205: remain separately reviewable designs, not hidden additions to this pass.
