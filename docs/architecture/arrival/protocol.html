Specification 01 · logical meaning

Arrival Protocol

A storage-independent contract for ordered custody: one lineage, one authoritative head, deterministic records, explicit admission, and rebuildable reads.

Draft v1 Normative full-break target 26 August 2026
01

Status and scope

This document defines the logical behavior of an Arrival ledger. It intentionally does not define files, SQL schemas, network endpoints, or query languages. Those are implementations of the Backend Contract or encodings defined by the Wire Format.

Normative language

MUST identifies a conformance requirement, SHOULD a requirement that may be departed from only with a documented reason, and MAY an optional behavior. This is a proposed target; the words do not retroactively claim that the branch already supplies every adapter.

Ratified

Ratified 26 August 2026 as the 1.0 full-break target (decision:design/arrival-backend-neutral-protocol), consistent with the five substrate laws of decision:design/arrival-substrate-laws. The wire profile this document references pins only after the seam triage of decision:design/arrival-wire-v1-seam-triage.

Goals

  • Keep logical history independent of its physical storage engine.
  • Make append order, atomicity, and lineage identity portable.
  • Allow deterministic migration, replication, audit, and export.
  • Support personal, team, embedded, and remote deployments without mandatory double storage.
  • Detect accidental damage locally and history rollback with an external witness.

Non-goals

  • Arrival does not make a fully compromised authority unable to destroy its own bytes.
  • Arrival does not prescribe a universal query schema or SQL dialect.
  • Arrival does not make wall-clock time a trustworthy ordering axis.
  • Arrival does not solve cross-lineage global ordering.
  • Arrival does not silently infer authority from a file suffix or database path.
02

Vocabulary and roles

lineage
An independently ordered history opened by exactly one genesis record.
record
The atomic logical custody unit at one dense ordinal in a lineage.
head
The tuple (lineage, ordinal, record_hash) naming a complete prefix.
authority
The one backend currently designated to assign new ordinals for a lineage.
replica
A backend that holds an exact prefix but does not independently assign coordinates.
admission
The policy gate that validates and authorizes authored content before custody is assigned.
projection
Rebuildable query state derived from a known ledger prefix.
witness
State retained outside the authority that remembers or attests a previously observed head.

Actors

Writer

Authors content

Submits a record draft and may sign its transport-stable content commitment.

Custodian

Admits custody

Applies policy, selects the authority, and requests an atomic append.

Reader

Consumes a prefix

Scans records or queries a projection with an explicit freshness watermark.

Name collision

The external Arrival witness in this suite is not the branch’s existing engine.witness.WitnessPosition, which is a read-path cursor over a store prefix. Both use observed position, but they have different jobs and wire identities. Ruled: the code vocabulary for the external artifact is head attestation; “witness” stays the read cursor (decision:design/arrival-suite-review-dispositions §3, Rule 18 glossary discipline).

03

The logical model

Lineage and coordinates

A genesis record opens a lineage at ordinal zero. Every successor uses the same lineage identifier and the next integer ordinal. A record coordinate is therefore (lineage, ordinal). It is stable across backends and exports.

A record may expand into several projected rows. The stable projected coordinate is (lineage, ordinal, sequence), where sequence is the zero-based position inside that record’s deterministic expansion. A batch does not consume one ledger ordinal per fact.

Core record kinds

KindRoleRequired behavior
genesisOpens the lineage and founding keyExactly ordinal 0; self-naming and self-certifying.
keyIntroduces an observer keySigned by a key already valid before its ordinal.
factCarries one canonical fact rowExpands deterministically to sequence 0.
batchCarries one atomic fact ceremonyExpansion order is part of its body grammar.
tickCarries one canonical tick rowNever participates in wall-clock ledger ordering.

The protocol treats record bodies as semantic JSON objects. The v1 wire profile pins the post-triage forms of the fact, batch, and tick object grammars used by the branch codecs (decision:design/arrival-wire-v1-seam-triage). Storage backends MAY normalize those values internally only if they preserve the JSON value exactly enough to reproduce commitments and canonical export.

Three different commitments

Content commitmentCovers authored fields and remains stable when another custodian assigns a coordinate.
Record hashCovers the full custody envelope except itself, including coordinate, predecessor, and optional signature.
Head attestationCommits a complete prefix outside the mutable authority and detects rollback or re-chaining.
04

Required invariants

  1. Single genesis. Ordinal 0 is a valid genesis; no later record has kind genesis.
  2. Dense order. Ordinals begin at 0 and increase by exactly 1 with no gaps or repeats.
  3. One lineage. Every record in a scan belongs to the genesis lineage.
  4. Hash continuity. Genesis has no predecessor; every successor names the preceding record hash.
  5. Canonical integrity. Every stored record hash recomputes under the selected wire profile.
  6. Atomic record. A reader observes all of a record or none of it.
  7. Atomic record. Every authority atomically appends at least one record; an advertised multi-record transaction commits its complete suffix or none of it.
  8. Durable success. A successful append survives the backend’s declared failure model.
  9. One authority. Only the designated authority assigns new coordinates for a lineage.
  10. Deterministic prefix. Reading through a captured head always yields the same records or a corruption/fork refusal.
No silent repair

A complete interior record that fails grammar, density, lineage, or hash checks is corruption. A conforming implementation MUST refuse it; it MUST NOT skip, rewrite, or truncate history in the name of repair.

05

State transitions

Mint

mint creates a new lineage with a signed genesis and returns head zero. Publication MUST be exclusive: two concurrent mints cannot overwrite one another or accidentally open the same physical target as different lineages.

Admit and append

The authority accepts one or more record drafts plus an expected head, up to its advertised atomic limit. Under one serialization boundary it compares the expected head, assigns dense coordinates, links and hashes the records, durably commits the suffix, and returns the committed records and new head. A mismatch refuses without mutation.

Logical operationpseudocode
append(expected_head, drafts):
  atomically:
    actual = head()
    require actual == expected_head
    require this backend is authority(actual.lineage)

    records = assign_and_chain(actual, drafts)
    validate(records)
    durable_commit(records)

  return Commit(records, head=records[-1].head)

The expected token MUST include the record hash as well as the ordinal. Comparing only the ordinal cannot distinguish two same-height forks.

Replicate exact records

Replication does not re-coordinate or re-sign records. A replica accepts only the exact next record, or an exact next group when it advertises transactional groups, for its current head. If lineage, ordinal, predecessor, or hash differs, it reports a fork.

Scan and capture

capture_head returns an immutable prefix token. scan(after, through) yields deterministic records strictly after one coordinate and no later than the captured head. A scan MUST NOT quietly cross onto a new head while it is in progress.

Export

Export is a read over a captured prefix encoded in the portable wire format. Export creates a replica artifact, not a second authority. A deterministic manifest names the exported head, record count, codec, and digest.

06

Authorship and admission

Arrival separates what an observer authored from where a custodian placed it, and it uses two different signatures to do so. The outer record signature is the arrival-content signature, and it is per-lineage: it covers the content commitment—kind, authored timestamp, observer, origin, and body—but not lineage, ordinal, predecessor, or record hash, and it is minted only in the lineage where the record first arrived.

content = SHA-256( JCS({ "k", "at", "observer", "origin", "body" }) )

The signature that travels through re-custody is the inner one. A fact row carries its own fact-domain signature inside body, over (kind, ts, observer, origin, payload), and that claim rides verbatim into every lineage that admits the row — which is what makes admission legal without anyone fabricating an author signature. The outer signature does not make the trip: a merge appends records the target custodian did not sign, holds no key for a foreign observer, and so drops the outer signature rather than re-minting it. Reading the outer signature as the author's portable claim is the one mistake this section exists to prevent (decision:design/arrival-wire-v1-seam-triage, ruling 5).

Genesis introduces the founding public key and is self-certifying. A key record introduces a later key only when signed by a key valid for its observer at a lower ordinal. Verifiers resolve keys from lineage history rather than a mutable side table.

Admission policy

Ledger validity and application admissibility are distinct. The backend enforces ledger invariants. The admission layer additionally MAY require ordinary signatures, authorize a team or observer, reject duplicate fact IDs, validate domain schemas, enforce size limits, and apply retention rules. Replication verifies existing custody; it does not rerun policy as though the source record were newly authored.

Remote service rule

Team authorization belongs at admission, ahead of the authoritative append. It is not implemented by granting clients direct INSERT access to ledger tables.

07

Reads, snapshots, and projections

The ledger is the custody truth; projections are query accelerators. A backend may query directly from canonical record storage, maintain normalized tables in the same transaction, or catch up asynchronously. The choice is local, but freshness is not allowed to become ambiguous.

Read result fieldMeaning
lineageThe ledger axis against which the result was resolved.
captured_headThe authority head captured for this read.
projected_throughThe greatest record head completely represented in the query surface.
rowsResults evaluated against the stated projected prefix.

A caller that requires current reads asks for projected_through == captured_head, waits for catch-up, or receives a ProjectionBehind refusal. It must never be handed stale rows labeled as current. Cross-lineage aggregation has no shared Arrival order; an aggregate query must report a per-lineage watermark map.

DuckDB without duplicated canonical storage

A DuckDB backend can store the canonical envelope as typed columns plus semantic JSON and expose views over that same data. It need not keep a parallel .arrival file. If extra fact tables are materialized for speed, they are projections and may contain only the fields and indexes that justify their cost.

08

The migration sidecar boundary

The full-break design gives legacy interpretation to one disposable sidecar. The new runtime has no “old store mode.” The sidecar implements a frozen LegacySource and writes through the same ArrivalSink any ordinary importer uses.

One genesis grammar

The sidecar mints an ordinary signed target genesis under the migration custodian. It does not introduce a second keyless or containment-only genesis into the core protocol. Source fingerprints, counts, exceptions, and equivalence claims live in the signed migration report and bootstrap witness receipt; a future in-ledger provenance record, if wanted, uses a namespaced extension kind.

Sidecar obligations

  • Read a stable source snapshot without mutating it.
  • Declare the source format and migration tool version.
  • Map every in-scope canonical row exactly once and in the ruled source order.
  • Preserve authored signatures and content where the old format carried them.
  • Produce source inventory, target head, exceptions, and deterministic equivalence evidence.
  • Be restartable: an interrupted stage is discarded or resumed only from a verified target head.
  • Publish the new store descriptor atomically after verification and witnessing.
No dual authority

During staging, the legacy store remains the user-visible authority and the target accepts only migration writes. At cutover, the descriptor changes once. If either side accepts ordinary writes concurrently, the result is two histories, not a migration.

Legacy conversion versus backend movement

The sidecar conversion above is only for a pre-Arrival source. Moving an existing Arrival lineage from the file backend to DuckDB or PostgreSQL is exact replication followed by the re-custody procedure: it preserves genesis, ordinals, signatures, record hashes, and the witnessed head rather than admitting the content into a new lineage.

As-built (Slice 4)

The offline migration sidecar in libs/migrate realizes this boundary:

  • Lineage-named staging: Writes to <store_dir>/<lineage>.arrival before atomic cutover, enabling safe resume or discard.
  • Custody domain separation: Genesis, key introduction, and report signatures are sealed under the arrival domain loops-arrival-v1.
  • Outer-unsigned migrated records: Legacy fact/batch/tick records retain original content and inner signatures verbatim; hash chaining binds them into the ledger.
  • Signed migration report beside target: A standalone JSON report (<store_dir>/<lineage>.migration-report.json) captures source fingerprints, target head, exceptions, and custodian Ed25519 signature.
  • 5 publish preconditions & apps already-migrated guard: Preconditions (source unchanged, target Full verify, equivalence re-run, inventory equality, journal bootstrap mint) must pass before surgical descriptor edit in .vertex; an apps-layer pre-flight refuses re-migrating existing arrival targets.
09

Correction, erasure, and re-custody

Correction

A semantic mistake is corrected by appending a new fact or explicit tombstone whose meaning supersedes earlier content. Editing or deleting an admitted record is not a correction operation; it is a custody integrity failure.

Erasure

Append-only history and legal or privacy erasure can conflict. Protocol v1 leaves the erasure profile open rather than pretending tombstones erase bytes. Candidate profiles include payload encryption followed by key destruction, lineage re-custody into a redacted successor with an external transition statement, and explicitly destructive compaction that creates a new lineage. Each changes what can be proven and requires a separate ruling.

Authority transfer

A portable export alone never transfers authority. A future re-custody ceremony MUST identify the old witnessed head, the new backend identity, an activation condition, and proof that the old authority will refuse new appends. Until that profile is ratified, operational transfer is a controlled stop-export-verify-switch procedure.

10

Versioning and extensions

Four version axes are independent even when their first value is all “1.”

AxisChanges whenMust not imply
ProtocolLogical semantics or invariants changeA physical schema rewrite everywhere
WireEnvelope grammar, canonicalization, or framing changesA new backend implementation
Backend schemaOne adapter changes its physical representationA change to portable records
APIClient operations or transport endpoints changeA rewrite of stored history

Core record kinds are unqualified lowercase names. Extension kinds SHOULD use a collision-resistant namespace such as org.example/name. A backend may preserve an unknown extension record without projecting it, but it MUST NOT claim full application conformance for semantics it does not understand.

Minimum protocol test suite

  • Mint racing and duplicate-genesis refusal
  • Concurrent compare-and-append with one winner
  • Atomic multi-record append under injected failure
  • Dense ordinal, lineage, predecessor, and record-hash corruption vectors
  • Exact replication and same-height fork vectors
  • Snapshot isolation while another writer advances the head
  • Cross-backend export byte equality under the selected wire profile
  • Projection rebuild equality and explicit behind-watermark behavior
  • Witness rollback and history-rewrite vectors
Open before ratification

Ratification still needs the authority-transfer profile, the erasure posture, the extension-kind policy, and executable cross-language conformance vectors. The storage-independent split itself does not depend on those choices.
